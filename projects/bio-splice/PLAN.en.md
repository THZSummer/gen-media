**12 sub-themes × 5 periods = 60 periods**; **all delivered** (135 finals). For the project-wide summary see [`SUMMARY.md`](SUMMARY.en.md).

> **Plan revision (2026-10-04, after dragon · nine resemblances was completed)**: this sub-theme revealed two hard constraints in measurement,
> and the period content of the following 10 sub-themes was re-ordered accordingly:
> ① **Head parts (head/beak/face/eye swaps) cannot be transplanted**—the base's species noun is both a placeholder and an anchor:
> keep it and the transplant piece cannot get in, delete it and the donor takes over the whole animal. **All periods such as "fish with a beak", "deer with a crest", "carnivorous claw" have been rewritten.**
> ② **A part needs a bearing structure**—claws need limbs, wings need a wing base; no limb parts on plants/snakes/fish.
> See §5 rules 73–78 and the "mechanism prediction" column of each sub-theme table.

## 1. Project Positioning

Graft **A's organs onto B's body** and shoot it in the realistic language of documentary photography (or microscopy / specimen imaging), as if it were a species that really exists.

**Not limited to animals**—plants, fungi, algae and lichens can all be base or donor. Three groups:

- **Group A · cross-species (animal × animal)**: organ transplants between two animals. The most mature group; most mechanism findings come from here.
- **Group B · cross-kingdom splicing (animal × plant × fungus)**: crossing the animal/plant/fungus kingdoms. Two of the hooks come from organisms that "are themselves composites" in the classics.
- **Group C · part atlas (cross-species rotation of the same organ)**: a fixed base, adding the same kind of organ in turn, making an "atlas" volume.
  Note: it can only be an **"add into an empty slot"** kind of addition, never "replace the base's own organ of the same name"—the latter is a same-material replacement, measured to reach only partial (rule 67).

> **The lesson of this sub-theme (rules 89–91)**: the neck/legs are **the same shape** as the base's (neck for neck, legs for legs),
> so only the **shape changes, never the material**; freeing the placeholder **neither adds nor subtracts** for canonical structures (R2/R3 control).
> The only piece recognizable at a glance as a transplant is the **differently shaped** tail-feather fan.
> → **When picking donors, prefer "differently shaped" pieces** (antlers, ears, feather fans, coils), not pieces of the same name but a different species.

> **The value of this sub-theme is that it gives the master criterion for "transplant difficulty" (rule 92)**:
> the more the base looks like "an already fixed species", the harder the transplant piece;
> the more the base looks like "a lump of raw material" (mycelium), the easier the transplant piece.
> This unifies 83/87 (canonical structures), 90 (an empty slot is not sufficient) and 91 (different shapes first).

  **it is not about turning the flower into a bird, but about letting the flower "grow a bird's feather from its heart"**: the mechanism limit gave back a better composition.

## 5. The Mechanisms Behind the Plan (from measurements on 14 delivered periods)

| Rule | Content | Significance for the plan |
|------|------|--------------|
| 56 | Transplant success depends on **whether the base description contains a placeholder** for that part | The base description must deliberately leave the target part blank (e.g. write `four stout legs` rather than `clawed` for the lizard base) |
| 62 | **A part needs a bearing structure** | Claws/paws must use a base with limbs, wings must use a base with a wing base; in the plan no limb part is ever put on a snake/fish |
| 57 | What is dangerous is **naming a whole as a noun**, not mentioning a species | Transplant pieces are always written `X's <part>`, never `X's body` |
| 59 | **At most two stacked in the same region**; cross-region stacking is allowed | For each period's part layout, first count the regions (head ≤2), then count the total number of pieces |
| 66 | A placeholder can be **freed** (delete the corresponding word from the base description) | Every "already occupied" part is marked "needs freeing" in the table |
| 67 | Topic priority: **add into an empty slot > free a placeholder > same-material replacement** | Same-material replacements (mycelial covering, visible algal layer, beast hide texture) are expected to reach only partial success; **then filter once more by 90/91: among empty slots, pick "differently shaped, highly recognizable" pieces** |
| 70 | **Constant within a period + different across periods** | Each period specifies its own "habitat · light · camera · canvas", guaranteeing that the 5 periods each have an identity |
| **73** | ❌ **Head parts (head/beak/eye swaps) cannot be transplanted**: the base's species noun is **both a placeholder and an anchor** | **No sub-theme may write "a fish with a beak", "a deer with a crest" or "a face swap" into its final targets**; this table's phrasings have been re-ordered accordingly |
| **74** | **A part needs a bearing structure** (a strengthening of rule 62) | Claws need limbs, wings need a wing base; **no limb parts on plants / snakes / fish**, no wings on bases without a wing base |
| **75** | **Small parts (eyes) have no available technique**: freeing the eye slot still does not land | No period selling "eyes" as its highlight; for this reason the atlas group's `eye-atlas` was rewritten as `horn-atlas` |
| **76** | **Only one base per round** | A same-round control is valid only for shots with the **same base**; shots mixing bases must be given their own round to supply controls, otherwise the objective metrics are unusable |
| **77** | **Freeing a placeholder is not a master key**: it works only for parts where the base's prior is weak | Do not expect "deleting the description frees it" for the head/face; freeing works only for peripheral parts such as claws and scales |
| **78** | **Judging "empty slot/occupied" in the plan requires checking the base text word by word** | D3 rabbit eyes were once misjudged as "add into an empty slot"; the base actually wrote `dark lidless eyes` |
| **79** | **A pose sentence must stay silent about the target part** | Whatever the pose mentions, **the base control grows it too** (R11: the pose said "wings spread" → the control cat grew wings as well, voiding the whole round) |
| **80** | **The presentation layer is not neutral**: camera/canvas change the transplant result, and light decides whether a fine part can be seen | Habitat/time may be changed, but **the camera and the subject's relative size in frame must not be changed casually**; fine line-like parts need a dark background + side light (R10/R14 cat tail, R12 whiskers) |
| **81** | **Spatial-relation clauses fail; switch to a noun phrase** | `X wrapped around Y` is a no-op, `X coils around Y` lands—**the relation between part and base must be written plainly too** (turtle-snake's N3 coil) |
| **82** | **When something "should have worked but did not", suspect the phrasing first, the placeholder second** | N3 was ranked "easiest" by mechanism yet landed 0%; only the sentence structure had to change |
| **83** | **Whether freeing a placeholder works depends on how much the base depends on that part** | Deleting the turtle's neck/tail does not hurt the base → fully successful; deleting cat paws, snake scales or fish fins hurts credibility → only partial or 0% (two sides of the same thing as 87) |
| **84** | **Multiple takes are a low-cost repeatability check** | Render 2–3 images with different seeds under the same presentation; only if all succeed may one say "this part is stable" (turtle-snake periods 01/02) |
| **85** | ⚠️ **Habitat is a hard constraint**: when a transplant piece conflicts with the scene's semantics, the model **keeps the scene and drops the part** | **Choose a compatible habitat first, then discuss phrasing and placeholders**—this ranks before the phrasing discipline. Evidence: same base, same sentence, 0% underwater / 100% out of water (fish-bird) |
| **86** | **Scene compatibility is judged per region** | Within one image, the exposed part can grow a piece and the part under water cannot (fish-bird period 02) |
| **87** | **Canonical structures cannot free a placeholder** | Fish fins / cat paws / snake scales / deer neck and legs are restored by the model even when deleted from the description; this contrasts with the turtle's neck and tail (deleting them does not hurt the base) (two sides of the same phenomenon as 89) |
| **88** | **A "single usable part" can carry a sub-theme too** | Use the **presentation layer's independent variable** as the between-period difference (fish-bird's stages of leaving the water); there is no need to force a new part every period |
| **89** | **Canonical structures: the shape can be changed, the material cannot** | Deer neck/legs, fish fins, cat paws and snake scales all belong here; **freeing a placeholder neither adds nor subtracts for them** (deer-crane R2 vs R3 control) |
| **90** | **An empty slot is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one** | The donor piece's **recognizability** also matters: the deer tail is the only truly empty slot, yet the crane tail feathers hit only 1/4 (1 of 4 seeds) |
| **91** | **Donor priority narrows further: different shape > same shape** | Antlers/ears/feather fans/coils are recognizable at a glance; neck-for-neck, leg-for-leg and scale-for-scale only yield the same animal "slender/longer or deformed" |
| **92** | 🎯 **The base's morphological freedom determines transplant difficulty** (master criterion) | The more fixed the base (deer/fish) → the harder the transplant; the more the base looks like raw material (mycelium) → the easier. **83/87/90/91 are all corollaries of it** |
| **93** | **Scale is part of the presentation layer too** | Macro subjects require replacing the whole framing sentence and photography layer (`macro photograph, focus-stacked`); CN/photography templates cannot be reused across scales |
| **94** | **Presentation can rescue a weak period** | When a part is "invisible" (internal structure/same material), change the presentation first (section, lift-open, lighting): the algal-layer period thus went from the hardest to the best (5.00 ×2) |
| **95** | **If the control already does half of it, points must be deducted** | When the base already has a certain form, the marginal contribution of the transplant piece is evaluated honestly (fruticose lichen 4.55 rather than 5.00) |
| **96** | 🎯 **What can be freed is an "attribute"; what cannot is a "structure"** | Body-surface texture/colour/pattern can be deleted (cordyceps MY1: freed version A=4 vs occupied version A=3); organs and limbs cannot (89/87). **96 and 92 complement each other**: 92 looks at how fixed the base as a whole is, 96 at whether the spot you want to free is structure or attribute |
| **97** | **"Pieces growing from the body surface" need no bearing structure** | A stroma only needs the "body surface" as a face to grow from (cordyceps 6 images at 5.00); a claw, by contrast, must have limbs. **When picking a piece, first ask: what bearing surface does it need?** |
| **98** | **Changing the presentation medium = a low-cost upgrade for the finale period** | Ecological photo → specimen photo, not one word of the part changed, and the deliverable's identity turns from "photographed" into "collected" (cordyceps period 05) |
| **99** | 🎯 **The criterion rests on the "landing site", not the "whole"** | The Venus flytrap as a whole is highly fixed (unfavourable by 92) yet all three pieces succeeded, because the landing sites (margin/leaf surface/cavity) are not canonical structures. **92 is the whole view, 99 the local view** |
| **100** | **The third class of "freeing a placeholder": boundary appendages ≈ attributes** | Structures (organs, limbs) cannot be freed (87/89), attributes (texture, colour) can (96), **and boundary appendages (margin teeth/margin hairs/serrations) can be freed as well** |
| **101** | ⚠️ (hypothesis · unverified) **For cross-kingdom pieces the "seam" decides credibility** | A lone eyeball growing on a plant easily reads as "stuck on"; adding "socket/lid/seam" may raise the C score—left for later verification |
| **102** | **Changing the base requires re-validating the hit rate** | The same phrasing scores 5/5 on magnolia and 1/2 on lily (flower-bird period 04) → "the phrasing has been validated" ≠ "it works everywhere" |
| **103** | **Read the phrasing together with the landing site** | The "covering form" works on the cordyceps insect body (attribute) and fails completely on the flower's petal ring (structure) |
| **104** | **Mechanism limits often give a better composition** | Petals cannot be swapped out → the landing site retreats to the flower centre → the result reads *more* like "flower-and-bird". **A limit is not a loss, it is a design constraint** |
| **105** | 🎯 **Two independent thresholds: is the landing site a canonical structure + what bearing surface does this piece need** | Beast feet fail both (the root system is a structure + feet need joints) → 0%; beast horns pass both → 5.00. **Both questions must be asked when picking a piece** |
| **106** | **Whether changing the base lowers the hit rate depends on the nature of the landing site** | Attribute replacement and empty-face additions depend little on base morphology (old tree→standing dead wood still works); landing sites needing geometric cooperation are sensitive (magnolia→lily 5/5→1/2) |
| **107** | 🎯 **The donor's semantic category must match the landing site's function** | "Add a pair of wings on the back": insect membranous wings / bat skin wings / flying-fish fins (carrying flight semantics of their own) all work; **fish pectoral fins 0%, seed wings partial**—looking like it is not enough, **the semantics must be right** (the part-landing-site version of rule 85) |
| **108** | **Atlas-type sub-themes need a "baseline part"** | Part 1 holds no transplant piece; it is both the first page and the reference frame for the four later parts (wing-atlas and horn-atlas are isomorphic) |
| **109** | 🎯 **Thresholds can be assembled on purpose** | Align "landing site is an empty face (99) + needs no bearing structure (97) + needs no freed placeholder (96) + semantics match (107)" all at once → **zero failures** (all six horns of horn-atlas succeeded) |
| **110** | **Form can compensate for a small semantic deviation** | The narwhal's tusk is anatomically a tooth, not a horn, but the form "a single upward spiral" was accepted by the model |
