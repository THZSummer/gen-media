## 1. Why this supplementary round

In R4, `deer-cranetait` (seed 7101) landed: **a ring of white crane tail feathers grows out of the deer's rump**, while seed 7102 did not. Period 03 needs two finals, so two more seeds were tried.

## 2. Results: only one of three seeds succeeded

| Camera | seed | Result | Score |
|------|------|------|------|
| `deer-cranetait` | 7101 | ✅ **White feather fan** (same seed as R4, reproduced pixel for pixel) | **5.00** |
| `deer-cranetait-c` | 7103 | ❌ The rump shows only the deer's own white patch | 3.35 (A=1) |
| `deer-cranetait-d` | 7104 | ❌ Same as above | 3.35 (A=1) |

Counting 7102 from R4, **one success out of four seeds = a 1/4 hit rate**.

## 3. Period 03 has only 1 final

The project convention is 2–3 pieces per period, but here **one image is preferable to padding the count**:
the two ❌ images are not "weak finals" but cases where **the transplant never happened at all** (the white on the rump is the deer's own rump patch), and by discipline they cannot enter the period directory.

## 4. Takeaways

90. **An empty slot is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one**: the G6 tail feathers are the only "true empty slot" in the whole set (the deer's tail is too short to be seen), and even there the hit rate is only 1/4.
    Rule 67 (adding into an empty slot > vacating a slot > same-material replacement) needs an extra layer:
    **also look at the distinctiveness of the donor part** — strongly distinctive parts (feather fan, antlers, ears, coiling body) can succeed;
    parts shaped like the base's own (neck for neck, legs for legs, scales for scales) can only change shape, not material.
