## 2. The first time a base swap "held steady instead of dropping" (a supplement to Rule 102)

Period 04 swapped the base from an **old tree** to **standing dead wood** (cracked dead trunk + exposed roots), and both K1 + K4 **still held**.
Control, flower-bird: when switching magnolia → lily, the hit rate dropped from 5/5 to 1/2.

> **Conjecture**: it relates to the **nature of the landing site** — **attribute replacement (beast-hide texture) and empty-surface addition (beast horns) depend little on base form**;
> whereas flower-bird's hard case is "growing a cluster of plumes from the flower center", which is more sensitive to the flower's geometry (depth of the flower center, petal shape).

## 3. Honest labeling in the closing period

Of the three parts in Period 05, **only two landed** (beast hide + beast horns); the beast feet did not land —
the finals are scored at A=4, and the period README states explicitly "two of three parts work".

## 4. Takeaways

106. **Whether swapping the base lowers the hit rate depends on the nature of the landing site**: attribute/empty-surface landing sites are steadier,
     while landing sites that need geometric alignment (the flower center) are more sensitive. → When swapping the base, prefer a base with the "same landing-site nature".
