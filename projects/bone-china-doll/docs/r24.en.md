## Face close-up crop: the decisive evidence

`out/macro-skin/skin-face-r24.png` (cropped 470×570 from the 1600×2144 base image, then ×2):

- ✅ **The skin finally has a genuine porcelain-glaze texture**: from the cheekbones to the forehead there is a lustrous glaze sheen, and the light-to-dark transition is like a glazed porcelain surface;
- ✅ Eye detail is excellent: radial iris fibres, pupil, catchlight, upper and lower eyelashes each distinct, eyelid folds;
- ❌ **The surface has no detail at all** — no glaze bubbles, no polishing marks, no pinholes, just a porcelain surface **so smooth it looks like a 3D render**;
- ❌ Orange translucency at the ear rim (a by-product of the pointed ears).

> **This is exactly what the user meant by "not detailed enough"**: it is not "no porcelain feel" but "**the porcelain feel is there, the porcelain surface's microscopic detail is not**".

## Key conclusions

1. **Position is weight** (R11/R15 verified again): the same material text, moved from last place to first, goes from "no glaze"
   to "obvious glaze". This rule now has 3 independent pieces of evidence.
2. **`translucency ... of the ears` is executed literally as "pointed ears"**: the model understands "the ear is thin and translucent" as
   a physiological modification. **When describing translucency, do not name the ears**.
3. **"Fine" ≠ "smooth"**: I wrote `microscopic glaze texture / microscopic bubbles / polishing marks`,
   but they were overridden by `absolutely no CGI smoothness` and the overall semantics of a "perfect porcelain surface", and the model gave a **clean, smooth** surface rather than one **with traces**.
   → You must **list the porcelain surface's "defects" by name** (pinholes, craters, glaze bubbles, polishing marks, orange-peel ripple, uneven glaze colour),
   and **explicitly forbid a uniform, smooth surface**.

## Changes for the next round (R25)

1. **Cure the pointed ears**: in the material passage, remove `of the ears` and change it to `of the fingertips and the temples`;
   add `pointed ears / elf ears / glowing orange ears / 尖耳 / 精灵耳` to the negative
2. **Cure "too smooth"**: **name the fired glaze's real defects item by item** —
   `microscopic pinholes where the glaze pulled back` / `trapped bubbles and blisters under the glaze` /
   `faint polishing marks and hairline drag lines` / `a fine orange-peel ripple` /
   `glaze very slightly uneven in thickness and colour` / `tiny specks of kiln grit`,
   and add `real variation everywhere, never a uniform flat surface`
3. **Give the skin more pixels**: switch the face close-up to a **2048×2048 square canvas (4.19 MP)** — R14 already proved 8GB can run 4.92 MP.
   A square canvas makes the face larger, so cropping can truly become "microscopic".
