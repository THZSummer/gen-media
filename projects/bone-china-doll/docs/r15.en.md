# Round 15 (R15) · Composition diversity: replace wording with the **canvas aspect ratio**

> 🌐 Language: **English** | [中文](r15.md)

> This round solves the project's **only genuinely unsolved defect**: all three R10/R13 images are near-identical busts.
> Conclusion: **composition is controlled by canvas shape; viewpoint cannot be controlled by wording.**

## Background: why we no longer try wording

| Round | Approach | Result |
|------|------|------|
| R10 | `extreme close-up / macro` wording | ❌ All three pulled back to half-body |
| R11 | `ONLY ... fill the entire frame` extreme negative form | ✅ Cropping obtained; ❌ the cost was deletion of words and a quality collapse |
| R13 | Cropping up front + full material vocabulary | ❌ Still pulled back to half-body |

→ The wording route has been disproved three times. R14 provided a new clue: **changing resolution at the same seed rearranges the composition**.

## Experimental design

The baseline is still `BASE_Q10` (the authoritative quality baseline confirmed in R13), and the resolution returns to the cost-effective 1280-wide level,
**changing only the canvas aspect ratio**:

| # | Name | Size | Aspect ratio | Intent | seed |
|---|------|------|--------|------|------|
| 1 | full-figure | 1024×2048 | **1:2 ultra-portrait** | Force a full figure (tiara to porcelain slippers) | 1500 |
| 2 | back-view | 1280×1712 | 3:4 | First try at a **back / three-quarter back** camera position | 1510 |
| 3 | seated-square | 1600×1600 | **1:1 square** | Force a seated pose + both hands on the knees | 1520 |

## Key conclusions

> **The canvas aspect ratio is an effective composition controller; viewpoint/cropping wording is ineffective.**
>
> - ✅ 1:2 ultra-portrait → full figure; 1:1 square → seated/medium shot
> - ❌ `seen from behind`, `extreme close-up`, `ONLY ... fill the frame` → all ignored
>
> **Speculated mechanism**: the aspect ratio changes the geometry of the latent variables, and the model **can only** fill the canvas with it, so the composition is
> forcibly rearranged; wording is merely a semantic condition, and the model can "choose to disobey". This is consistent with R10's lesson —
> a long clothing description prioritises "photographing the whole magnificent outfit" and thereby overrides any cropping request.

## Bonus deliverable this round: **true macro (cropping method)**

The true macro that R10/R11/R13 could not obtain through the prompt was solved in one go this round by **cropping**,
the script is [`make_macro.sh`](../make_macro.sh), and the source image is the R14 3.43 MP final (the highest bytes/px in the whole project):

| Artifact | Content |
|------|------|
| `out/macro/macro-tiara.png` | True macro of the tiara: silver filigree scrollwork, milled bead edging, prong-set diamonds, faceted sapphires; **and the eyes are fully in frame** (iris/pupil/catchlight/eyelashes) → completely sidesteps R11's "macro with no eyes" pitfall |
| `out/macro/macro-wrist.png` | True macro of the wrist ball joint: the joint ball, **the internal metal tension ring**, crisp glaze highlights, gold-thread brocade and lace edges |

> **Methodology**: when a composition is systematically rejected by the model at the semantic layer, **switch to the geometric layer (canvas/cropping) to solve it**
> rather than adding more words. This is the project's most reusable lesson.
