## This round's hypothesis (retrospective verdict: **the hypothesis itself was wrong**)

The reasoning at the time was: every BASE from R7 to R11 contained `pristine / flawless / perfectly smooth fired glaze /
completely clean`, and these "perfection words" suppress physical texture, which is why the face looked like an airbrushed
wax figure. So R12:

1. **Deleted all "perfection words"**, replacing them with **descriptions** of the physical behaviour of the glaze (glaze pooling, micro bubbles, polishing marks, translucency at the ear tips and fingertips)
2. Added `one crisp key light ... every highlight is small and sharp` (intent: small sharp highlights = a glassy feel)
3. Kept a full facial feature description in every image (fixing R11's "macro with no eyes" bug)
4. Simplified the hands from "two hands" to "**ONE hand**" (fixing R11's "ten fingers" bug)

## Key lessons (two, both reusable)

### 1. A standalone lighting instruction for "sharp highlights" invites **the fixture into the frame** (⚠️ this item was partially corrected by R13)

At the very end R12 wrote a standalone lighting instruction: `One crisp key light from the upper left so that every highlight
is small and sharp`, and the result was that image 3 **had a lamp appear in the upper-left corner of the frame** (with a second overexposed patch at the right edge).

> ⚠️ **R13's correction**: this cannot simply be attributed to the word `key light`. `BASE_Q10` **already contains**
> `one strong key light` (inside BASE_Q8's `Sculptural chiaroscuro studio lighting with one strong
> key light and deep natural falloff`), and neither R10 nor R13 **painted a fixture into the frame**.
>
> **The corrected rule**:
> - ❌ Do not **single out** lighting as its own instruction, and especially do not ask for "highlights small and sharp" — that is equivalent to asking for a **visible sharp strong light source**;
> - ✅ Blending light **into the scene description** (`soft north window light`, `sculptural chiaroscuro lighting with deep
>   natural falloff`) is safe, even if the words `key light` appear in it;
> - The project's old R4 conclusion still holds: **do not write specific fixture nouns** (`softbox / lamp / strobe`).

### 2. Describing "a glaze like glass / small sharp highlights" yields **wet plastic**, not fired glaze

`a fired glaze that behaves like real glass` + `bright crisp specular highlights` +
`every highlight is small and sharp` → what the model produced was **wet, oily, continuously smeared** highlights,
joining into large colour blocks on the forehead and chest, reading as "plastic that has been doused with water" or "undried clay", **more fake than R10**.
> **Rule**: the glaze feel of bone china comes from **soft and restrained reflection** (R10's window light achieved exactly this),
> not from "sharp highlights". To add texture, add **craft marks** (milled edges, polishing marks, fabric warp and weft),
> not stronger reflections.

### A third, incidental point: writing the eyes as a "focal subject" **enlarges the eyes**

After BASE_Q12 wrote `Open glass doll eyes of realistic size`, all three faces were **juvenilized** (big eyes,
round cheeks), and `oversized eyes` in the negative did not suppress it. R10's eyes were obtained through an
**overall proportion description** like `An elegant princess face with
refined adult proportions ... almond eyes of realistic size`.

## Revised direction (for R13)

1. **Return the quality baseline to `BASE_Q10` as-is** (no more inventing BASEs; first prove that the R10 level can be reproduced)
2. Only layer on **small increments already proven effective**, without touching "perfection words / fixture words / glass metaphors"
3. Stop separately emphasizing the eyes' "size/glass"; add only **a little** catchlight description inside the overall proportion sentence
4. True macro (never really solved in R10/R11) should instead be obtained by **cropping from the high-resolution final images**, no longer by forcing cropping through the prompt
5. For hands, reuse the verified R12 wording: **explicitly state "only one hand" + "exactly five fingers" + "a single segment per finger"**
