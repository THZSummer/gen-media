## 3. Two adjustments made per the rules

1. **Period 02's presentation was changed from "low camera close to the ground · portrait" to "wetland mud bank · eye-level full body"** (Rule 80).
   The PLAN originally specified a low-camera portrait format; on cat-eagle, "low-camera portrait format + terminal part (tail)" had painted the donor
   as another complete animal, so here we avoided it proactively.
   → Result: N2 lies flat on the mud, its scale row visible joint by joint, clearly better than R1's tilted tail on the stream stones (4.35).

2. **Each take uses a different seed, but the presentation layer is completely identical** (constant within a period, Rule 70):
   Periods 01/02 each produced three takes, to judge whether "holding up" was a lucky draw.
   → All three seeds held up, showing that **part-level transplant is reproducible on a base with the slot freed**.

## 4. Takeaways

83. **Whether freeing up a slot works depends on how much the base depends on that part**:
    deleting the turtle's neck and tail does not harm the base (a turtle minus them is still a turtle) → fully holds up;
    deleting cat-eagle's cat paws or dragon-nines' snake scales harms the base's credibility (the model restores them) → only partial or failure.
    This is a supplementary tiering of Rules 66/67.
84. **Multiple takes are a low-cost reproducibility check**: three images from the same presentation with different seeds,
    and only when all three hold up do you dare say "this part is stable".
