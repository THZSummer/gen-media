## 1. Design: this round does only one thing—completing the same-round control

The 4/5 images of R9 (antlers+ears / antlers+ears+scales) already held, but that round's same-round control was
`base-nospecies` (a base with no species name), **not the same base they used**,
so the objective metrics are not usable for those two.

This round adds the same-round control `base-snake`, leaving everything else (base, part, presentation, seed) **unchanged character for character**:

| Shot | Base | Part | Region distribution |
|------|------|------|----------|
| `base-snake` | snake (head/eyes complete) | — | control |
| `dragon-head` | snake | D1 antlers + D9 ears | head 2 |
| `dragon-head-scale` | snake (scale slot freed) | D1 + D9 + D6 scales | head 2 · body 1 |

The presentation follows Period 05's **S5**: misty-morning reeds · side-backlight outlining the silhouette · face-on close-up 200mm · square.

## 3. Takeaways

77. **A final round can "just add the control"**: R9's combination already held; what was missing was only an attributable baseline.
    Split "verifying the mechanism" and "producing finals" into two rounds: the former can cheaply trial-and-error,
    the latter is responsible only for completing the discipline (the same-round control)—**do not let one round carry too many purposes at once**.
78. **The framing sentence of a close-up period must change along with it**: the CN layer says 「全身像」 while the photographic layer writes `tight close-up`
    and they fight each other (already fixed in R8: `_styled(frame=…)`). The close-up is the only means in this sub-theme of pulling period identities apart
    just by changing the camera—full-body shots can only be distinguished by habitat/light.
