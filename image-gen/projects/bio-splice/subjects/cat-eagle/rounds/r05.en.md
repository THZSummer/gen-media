## 1. The problem

Periods 01–05 shared one presentation set (misty autumn meadow · overcast soft light · eye-level 600mm · square),
and the five periods read like "the same photo with swapped accessories". The fix is the same as `dragon-nines`' R5–R7:
**constant within a period + different between periods** (Rule 70), with the camera chosen per subject (Rule 71).

## 3. The three pitfalls (all of which became rules)

### 1. Camera/canvas change the transplant result (Rule 80)

The same sentence `a domestic cat's long ringed tabby tail`:

| Presentation | Result |
|------|------|
| R6 eye-level 600mm full-body shot | ✅ the cat tail grows on the eagle |
| R10/R14 low camera 85mm vertical | ❌ **an extra complete cat is drawn** (the donor was individualized) |
| R17 eye-level full body 400mm | ✅ holds again |

> Conclusion: **"changing only the presentation layer leaves the mechanism findings unchanged" must be narrowed**—
> habitat/time/canvas can change, but **the camera and the subject's relative size in frame must not change casually**;
> every camera change requires re-checking whether the part still lands.

### 2. A pose sentence must not mention the transplanted part (Rule 79)

R11's pose read "it spreads both wings to the maximum"—**the cat in the base control grew eagle wings too**.
Once the control is polluted, the whole round's objective metrics are void (this is the bottom line of "a control must differ by only one variable").

### 3. Light decides whether a fine part lives or dies

The same cat-whisker sentence: **unreadable** under midday hard light (R12), **clearly successful** under dark-background single-side light (R16).
→ Choose the light for the **part's material**: fine line-like parts need a dark background + side light, while solid parts can withstand hard light and backlight.

## 4. Consolidation

79. **A pose sentence must stay silent about the target part**: like the base description, it "occupies a placeholder"—
    whatever it mentions, the control grows too.
80. **The presentation layer is not neutral**: camera/canvas change the transplant result, and light decides whether a fine part can be seen.
    Changing the presentation requires re-checking "is the part still there, and can it be seen".
