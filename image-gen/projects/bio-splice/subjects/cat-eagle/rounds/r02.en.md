## 1. Audit: first confirm that what the user saw is real (verdict from magnified crops)

The diagnostic part chosen is the **feet and legs**: a cat paw (furry leg + paw pads) and an eagle talon (yellow scaled tarsus + black curved claw) cannot be confused,
and the difference in this part is unaffected by pose/camera. Method: use `ffkit.crop` on the same pixel box + ×2 magnification.

| R1 variant | Feet | Hindquarters | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `eaglecat-seamless` (A) | **Eagle talons** | pure eagle wing feathers | ❌ **Zero cat elements** |
| `eaglecat-surreal` (C) | (mean_abs_diff against A is only 9.5) | same as A | ❌ Zero cat elements |
| `eaglecat-seam` (B) | still eagle talons, but **fur on the legs** + a seam | mixed | ⚠️ A messy patchwork, not a clean splice |

> **Consistent with the user feedback**: 2 of the 3 are pure eagles, and the third is merely "an eagle that grew a bit of fur".
> The reverse direction (cat head + eagle body) succeeded in all three — so this is not an art-style problem.

**Methodological note**: global SSIM / PSNR **cannot** tell whether "the splice happened".
In the SSIM against the pure-eagle control, the successful and failed variants both fall between 0.73–0.82 (the whole image is dominated by the shared composition and background),
and the value shifts as soon as the pose changes. **The verdict must be based on local crops**.

## 2. R2: four single-variable hypotheses (baseline = the R1-A original sentence, changing only one thing each time)

| Hypothesis | Change | Result | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1 negation backfire** | Delete `, with no wings at all` | the image **did not change at all** (SSIM 0.8095→0.7956) | ❌ **Disproved** |
| **H2 word order** | swap the two head/body clauses (body first) | **the feet change from eagle talons to cat paws + tabby furry legs**, the body complete | ✅ **The decisive lever** |
| **H3 material scope** | append `Feathers grow only on the head and the neck; the whole body is covered in fur.` | still eagle talons | ❌ Ineffective on its own |
| **H4 stronger wording** | `golden eagle's` → `large bird of prey's` | still eagle talons, but the eagle head grows **ear tufts** (the cat's influence seeps into the head) | ❌ Ineffective on its own |

> ⚠️ **Correcting the R1 document's conclusion**: [r01.md](r01.en.md) §4 once listed "delete the wings clause" and
> "add mixing permission" as R2's primary recommendations — **the former has been disproved by R2**, and the latter (`assembled from
> two different animals`) **also had no visible effect** in this round's four tests.
> R1 was wrong to credit "mixing permission": what actually worked was **word order** (under same-seed controls, H2 took effect on its own).

### H2's side effect: two heads

Body-first made the cat's body appear complete, but **the cat's own head was still there**, and the eagle head became a second head stacked on top.

## 3. R3: fixing the two heads (positive restriction) — failed

| Variant | Change | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `neck-down` | `the body` → `the body from the neck down` | still two heads |
| `+mixing permission` | append "the two halves come from two different animals" | still two heads |
| `+four furry paws` | append `It stands on four furry paws with soft toe pads.` | still two heads |
| `+replace the eagle word` | stack the H4 word swap | **becomes single-headed**, but turns into a "cat face + beak" |

> A **scope restriction** like "from the neck down" cannot hold down a named animal.

---

## 4. R4: un-naming the body — failed (falls back to a pure eagle)

Hypothesis: the name "cat" in the body clause is itself summoning a whole cat; writing only parts (four striped furry legs / soft paws / a ringed tail) would
leave the body "no head to summon".

**Result: all fell back to a pure eagle** (including the head-first variant used as a control). The cat's body disappeared completely.

> **Key finding**: the name `cat` is **load-bearing**. Not naming it → the parts are not enough to summon a cat →
> the strong species (the eagle) eats the whole body. **So the "un-naming" route is dead.**

## 5. R5: three workarounds — all failed

| Variant | Idea | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `parts-of-cat` | bind the name to the part list: `the torso, flanks, four furry legs and long ringed tail **of** a domestic tabby cat` | still two heads |
| `one-head` | append a **positive count**: `a single animal with exactly one head:` | still two heads (**the count was not executed at all**) |
| `single-phrase` | merge into a single noun phrase, removing the `whose head … and whose body …` coordination structure | still two heads |

> The repo's old experience that "naming + counting is executed literally" **fails here**—
> body parts can be named and executed, but **a constraint on the overall structure such as "only one head" is not executed**.

---

## 6. Mechanism (derived from 5 rounds of same-seed controls)

1. **A named animal renders as a whole**:
   - named in the **head slot** → gives only one head (**which is why cat head + eagle body succeeded**)
   - named in the **body slot** → gives a whole animal, **its own head included** (→ two heads)
2. **Neither positive exclusion nor positive counting is reliable**: `no wings at all` (H1) and `exactly one head` (R5-2)
   both had no effect on the image. This is the hard limitation of Z-Image-Turbo using `ConditioningZeroOut`, which **has no negative prompt**.
3. **A strong species' whole-body prior eats the weak species' body**: eagle (wings/talons/feathers are a recognizable whole plan)
   vs cat (a generic quadruped + fur). Whoever is named as the body appears as a whole; if no one is named, the strong side takes all.

---

## 7. Conclusion and next steps

**Conclusion: on Z-Image-Turbo, "eagle head + cat body" cannot be cleanly spliced.** All four routes were tried:

| Route | Result |
|---|---|
| head first | pure eagle |
| body first | a complete cat + an appended eagle head (two heads) |
| un-named | pure eagle |
| name bound to parts / positive single-head count / remove coordination | still two heads |

Three feasible directions (ordered by cost-effectiveness):

| # | Direction | Method | Cost |
|---|------|------|------|
| **1** | **image-edit-comfyui (ControlNet)** | use R1's `pure-cat control` as the control image to **lock the cat's body structure**, letting the prompt change only the head. The structure is guaranteed by the control image, no longer by sentence pattern | fast (~10–60 s/image), and this is the **first use of that skill on a real project** |
| 2 | **Qwen-Image 2512** | use **true negatives** to exclude: `two heads, second head, cat head, cat ears, wings, feathers on body, talons` — exactly the scenario this repo has repeatedly confirmed as "exclusion constraints must go negative" | slow (6–13 min/image) |
| 3 | Shelve the direction | the user has already accepted the "cat head + eagle body"; concentrate the budget on it (finals, extending to owl head + rabbit body / snake body / deer body, the three-piece composite) | 0 |

## 8. Consolidation

51. **Judging "whether the splice happened" must come down to local crops**: whole-image SSIM/PSNR is dominated by the shared composition and background,
    with successful and failed variants in the same band; and the value drifts as soon as the pose changes. Pick a **part that cannot be confused** (e.g. cat paw vs eagle talon).
52. **"A named animal renders as a whole"**: the head slot gives only one head, the body slot gives a whole animal (its own head included).
    When making a "head-body splice of two animals", only **weak species as body + strong species as head** works; the reverse necessarily degenerates or produces two heads.
53. **Neither a positive exclusion sentence (`no X`) nor a positive count (`exactly one X`) is executed**—
    on an engine with no negative prompt, any "subtract something" requirement is unsolvable and the engine must be changed (a strengthened version of bone-china-doll lesson 46).
54. **"Un-naming" is not the way to fix "an extra part grew"**: the name is load-bearing; removing it makes the parts that should be there disappear too.
55. **Single variables and the same seed are the only reason this round could pinpoint "word order"**: R1 once attributed the causality to "mixing permission",
    and R2's four single-variable controls overturned that. **Attribution must rest on single-variable controls, not on observed correlation.**
