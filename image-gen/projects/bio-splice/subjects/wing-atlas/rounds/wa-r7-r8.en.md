## 2. Why these two are stronger than the "fish pectoral fins / samara wings"

- **The flying fish's long fins**: the word `flying-fish` itself carries the semantics of "flying", and the model has the prior that "flying fish glide with their fins";
- **The crane's white wings**: they are wings in themselves, and they are **different in both shape and colour** from the bird's own brown feathered wings —
  forming **double-layered wings** on the back, the most convincing image in this atlas.

In contrast, R4's fish pectoral fins (`pectoral fins`, with no flying semantics) **landed at 0%** —
**the same landing site and the same sentence pattern, with the only difference being the donor's semantic category** (Rule 107).

## 3. Wrap-up: the completeness of an "atlas"

Five sections = baseline + four kinds of second wing pair. Of the four, **three are fully established** (membranous wings, flying-fish fins, crane feathers),
one is partial (samara wings, not included in the finals) and one is negative (fish pectoral fins).
**The two that never landed are written truthfully into parts.md and do not enter the period directory.**
