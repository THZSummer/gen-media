## 3. Two lessons on approach

1. **A "pseudo cross-section" can rescue a period that is mechanically very weak**: period 04 was originally the hardest period of this sub-theme
   (the algal layer is hidden inside the lichen, in the "same-material/internal structure" tier). Once it was written as
   "the surface layer is peeled back, exposing the internal structure", the green cells and algal filaments became the protagonists of the frame directly,
   both scoring 5.00. → **Presentation is not just about making the image look good; it can change whether a part can be seen at all.**
2. **When the base already carries a form, deduct honestly**: the mycelium description already contained `branching threads`,
   so on the tundra it grew into coral-like branching on its own; the fruticose part only contributed "denser + white pruina".
   A is 4 rather than 5 — **the control had already done half the work**.

## 4. Takeaways

(Rules 92/93 are in [lc-r1.md](lc-r1.en.md).)

94. **Presentation can rescue a weak period**: when the part itself is "invisible" (internal structure, same-material replacement),
    change the presentation first (cross-section, peeling back, lighting), then consider swapping the part.
95. **When the control has already done half the work, deduct**: when the base carries a form on its own,
    assess the transplanted part's marginal contribution honestly (fruticose 4.55 rather than 5.00).
