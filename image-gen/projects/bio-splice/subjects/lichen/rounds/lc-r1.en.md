## 1. Following the process lesson, do the probe first

The rule bought by fish-bird's 11 wasted images is: **the first round of a new sub-theme starts with a minimal probe**.
This sub-theme's probe tests four things at once (same base, same seed, same presentation):

| Camera | Part | Result | Score |
|------|------|------|------|
| `base-mycelium` | — (control) | a radial mass of mycelium on the rock surface | — |
| `mycelium-crust` | CR1 crustose form | ✅ a cracked crustose lichen crust, radiating at the edges | **5.00** |
| `mycelium-algae` | AL1 green algal cells | ✅ **clusters of bright green algal cells embedded in the hyphae** | **5.00** |
| `mycelium-crust-algae` | CR1 + AL1 | ✅ form + algae in one frame = lichen itself | **5.00** |

**All four landed, not one wasted.**

## 2. Why it was so easy: the base is a mass of "no fixed shape"

Compare other bases under the same set of rules:

| Base | Morphological freedom | Result |
|------|-----------|------|
| deer / fish | low (neck, legs, fins are all **canonical structures**) | partial success / 0% |
| snake / turtle / lizard | medium | empty-slot parts hold up |
| **mycelium** | **high (a mass of hyphae with no "what it should grow into")** | **all hold up** |

→ **Rule 92: the less fixed the base's shape, the easier the transplanted part lands.**

## 3. Takeaways

92. **The base's morphological freedom determines transplant difficulty**: it unifies 83/87 (canonical structures cannot free up a slot),
    90 (an empty slot is not sufficient), and 91 (prefer a different form) into one simpler criterion —
    **first check whether the base is "fixed in shape", then check whether the part was chosen well.**
93. **Scale is part of the presentation layer**: here the animal sub-themes' "full-body shot + 600mm" was replaced by
    "macro close-up + 100mm macro + focus-stacked", swapping the framing sentence and the photographic layer together.
    The same CN/photography template **cannot be reused across scales**.
