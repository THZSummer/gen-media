## Two new lessons

### 1. A landscape canvas **cannot** force a lying pose

R15 proved "canvas shape can control composition" (1:2 portrait → full-body image), but R27 proves this rule **only holds in the portrait direction**:

| Canvas | Expected | Actual |
|------|------|------|
| 1:2 ultra-portrait (R15) | Full-body standing figure | ✅ Success |
| 2:1 landscape (R27) | Full-body lying on one's side | ❌ It only **widens the background**; the subject is still anchored on the face, producing a wide-frame bust |

> **Revised statement**: an ultra-**portrait** canvas can force a "taller subject" (a standing full body); an ultra-**landscape** canvas cannot force a "wider subject",
> because the model can fill both sides with background. **To get a lying full body you must rely on wording, not on the canvas.**

### 2. `warm translucency ... of the fingertips` makes the **hands glow**

This is **the same class of error** as R24's elf ears — name a body part and ask for "thin and translucent", and the model turns it into a **light source**:

| Round | Named part | Artifact |
|---|---------|------|
| R24 | `the ears` | Elf pointed ears + orange translucency |
| **R27** | `the fingertips` | **Fingers and palms emit orange light** (appears in both lying images; the fingers look like they are glowing) |

> **Rule upgrade (2nd verification)**: **do not name any body part for translucency**.
> Write something **location-unspecified** like `the thinnest parts of the porcelain`,
> and put `glowing hands / orange glowing fingers / 发光的手` into the negative.

## Key conclusions

1. **Makeup can be controlled precisely**: find "the sentence in the early position", rewrite it in place and add negatives, and one round fixes it.
   R26's problem was not "writing about blush" but writing the blush in the **highest-weight** position.
2. **The canvas lever is directional**: it can only stretch "taller", not "wider". This is an important qualification of R15's conclusion.
3. **Pose wording gets drowned out by face dominance**: when the base prompt is very long and the subject is a "princess portrait", the model prioritizes the face no matter how wide
   the canvas is. → You need to **shorten the base prompt + put the pose sentence at the very front** (R24's "position is weight").
