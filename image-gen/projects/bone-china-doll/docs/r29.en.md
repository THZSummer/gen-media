## Two changes this round

1. **Add three everyday moments**: half-reclining against a bolster holding tea / sitting hugging the knees on a mat by the window / dozing over a desk
2. **Restore the classical costume markers**: when lying in R28 the gauze spread into a flat surface, and the form of the **crossed collar, yingluo and belt** disappeared.
   R29 writes `an ivory silk robe with a crossed collar` + `a jade-and-pearl necklace` +
   `a jade-inlaid belt` into the short base prompt (Chinese: ivory crossed-collar fine silk / red-gold coiled-chi yingluo collar / gold-inlaid jade belt) — the short base prompt no longer has to give up clothing.
   The base prompt grows from R28's 635 characters to 776, still at a magnitude that "will not pull the camera in".

## Key conclusions

1. **A short base prompt can carry clothing detail**: R19 once concluded "wide composition ↔ clothing detail cannot be had together", R21 found the medium-density solution,
   and R29 further proves — adding **three named ornaments** (crossed collar / yingluo / jade belt) to a **pose-up-front** short base prompt
   does not affect the full-body composition at all. **The real enemy is always "the length of the exhaustive list", not "mentioning clothing"**.
2. **Once the pose sentence is up front, the pose reproduces reliably**: R28/R29 have 6 poses in total (lying on the side / prone / seated slantwise / half-reclining / hugging the knees / over a desk),
   **all landing on the first try**. This contrasts sharply with R27's 0/3, and the only difference is "pose up front + base prompt cut from 1802 to 635–776".
3. **Desk-type poses naturally lose the lower body**: not a failure but the inevitable framing of that pose. If you want "head to toe", do not choose a desk pose.
