## Two conclusions from this round

### Conclusion 1 (important): R10's quality comes from **the material vocabulary itself** and reproduces across seeds

Image 1 changed only the seed, with everything else character-for-character identical, and the result landed stably at the R10 level. This shows:

> The R11 / R12 degeneration is **not random fluctuation** but the **deterministic consequence of deleting the material vocabulary**.
> The project baseline is thereby confirmed: **`BASE_Q10` is the authoritative quality baseline**, and all later rounds make increments on top of it.

### Conclusion 2 (hard constraint): **material vocabulary volume and macro cropping cannot be had at the same time**

| Round | Material vocabulary | Cropping result |
|------|----------|----------|
| R10 | Complete (`BASE_Q10`) | ❌ All three pulled back to half-body |
| R11 | **Cut away** | ✅ True macro obtained (cost: quality collapse) |
| R13 | Complete (`BASE_Q10`) | ❌ Pulled back to half-body again |

Three experiments form a clean conclusion:

> **When a long, specific clothing/material description is present, Qwen-Image 2512 prioritises "photographing the whole magnificent outfit", and no "cropping up front / ONLY / no face" wording can override it.**
> Therefore "true macro" **should no longer be sought through the prompt** but obtained instead by **generating at high resolution and then cropping**.

## A **self-correction** to an R12 conclusion

The R12 document attributed "a fixture in frame" to the term `one crisp key light`. This round found a **counterexample**:
`BASE_Q10` **already contains** `one strong key light` (in the sculptural chiaroscuro passage from BASE_Q8),
yet neither the R10 nor the R13 results painted a fixture into the frame.

> **Revised statement**: what induces a fixture in frame is not the term `key light` itself, but more likely
> **pulling it out as a standalone lighting instruction and demanding "highlights are small and sharp"**
> (that is, demanding a visible, sharp, strong light source). It is safe only as **part of a light-quality description** such as `sculptural chiaroscuro lighting with one strong
> key light and deep natural falloff`.
> The conclusion still holds: **do not issue a standalone "sharp highlight" lighting instruction**, but there is no need to ban the words `key light`.

## Changes for the next round (R14)

1. **Lock the baseline to `BASE_Q10`** and stop inventing new BASEs
2. **Shift the main thrust to resolution/refinement** (the real bottleneck for detail richness):
   - Probe whether Qwen can run 1536×2048 on 8GB of VRAM (+50% pixels)
   - Or add a **hires-fix second-stage refinement** (upscale and resample at low denoise) → add microscopic detail without changing the composition
3. Get **true macro** by **cropping** the high-resolution final instead of writing it into the prompt
