## Key conclusions

1. **A form can be swapped like a part**: moving only one clothing sentence yields a whole different Eastern silhouette, while
   the face / hairstyle / jewellery / material / light are **all inherited stably**. This shows that the **layered structure of `BASE_E` works**,
   and expanding to any Han / Song / Ming form later requires replacing that same sentence only.
2. **`花钿` failed on all three attempts** (R17 generic context, R18 generic context, R19 Tang-style context):
   > **Subtle facial makeup decorations (forehead ornaments, cosmetic dimples and the like) are unreliable**; whereas **named objects** (yingluo, buyao, cloud collar,
   > gold-set jade belt, round fan, folding screen) land almost first try.
   > Conclusion: **whatever you want, give it an "object", not a "makeup detail".** From this round on, `花钿` is no longer attempted.
3. **The cost of the slim base**: to obtain the complete composition, the full figure deleted the clothing list, and the side effect is that the clothing **becomes plain**
   (the R19 full figure's skirt is almost without embroidery). This is a clear **trade-off**:
   > Wide composition ↔ clothing detail density cannot both be had within a single image; to have both, use a **division of labour across several images**
   > (the full figure gives the silhouette + the portrait/macro gives the craft).
