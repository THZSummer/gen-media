## Key conclusions

1. **"Lightness" is a physical property you can write directly; it need not rely on style words**:
   write out item by item "number of layers (several) + thickness (thin as cicada wings) + translucency (letting the inner layer's light and shadow through) + edge (crisp and clean, with the backbone of porcelain) +
   motion (floating as if about to lift, trailing like mist)", and the model will make the fabric into light thin gauze, rather than relying on vague style words
   like "flowing".
2. **The key to "half gauze, half bone china" is to give the cloth a "porcelain attribute"**: `crisp fine weave / cleanly cut edge /
   the stiffness of porcelain rather than the softness of cloth` — the gauze's translucency + the porcelain's crispness; only the two stacked together
   give that feel. Writing only "thin" yields ordinary thin gauze.
3. **Removing the dense gold weaving is necessary for "lightness"**: the semantics of gold weaving/brocade themselves carry thickness and weight;
   keeping only **sparse fine silver-thread embroidery** preserves detail without weighing it down.
4. These four rounds of this stage form a complete chain:
   **de-figurinization (R22) → Chinese/English division of labour restoring bone china (R23) → material up front to get the glaze (R24) →
   named defects to get micro detail (R25) → change only the garment to get half gauze half bone china (R26)**.
