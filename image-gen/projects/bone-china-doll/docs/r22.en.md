## Diagnosis: why she looks more and more like a figurine

Reviewing the first 21 rounds, the prompt kept reinforcing "she is an object":

| Wording | Effect |
|------|------|
| `handcrafted ball-jointed bone china doll` | the subject is literally a **doll** |
| `photographed like a real physical object on a table` | explicitly an **object** |
| `Visible ball-joint seams at the neck and wrists` | ball-joint seams = a **figurine trait** |
| `museum-vitrine realism` | a **display case** |
| `porcelain slippers` / `carved wooden stand` | a **display stand and accessories** |

So after three rounds of "Easternisation", the image quality got better and better, but she **looked more and more like a doll in a museum**.

## Three changes in this stage

1. **Completely delete** all words for doll / joints / display stand / display case from the positive, changing the subject to "an Eastern classical princess";
2. **Explicitly ban "figurine" in the negative** (`NEG_H` = `NEG_Q` + doll / ball joint / joint seam / display stand / display case / base / statue, in both Chinese and English);
3. **Switch the prompt to Chinese**, using the brushwork of a Dream of the Red Chamber character entrance to **lay out baimiao layer by layer**:
   hairstyle → face → brows → eyes → nose → lips → skin → jewellery → clothing → expression → environment → light → camera.
   > Rationale: Qwen-Image's text encoder is **Qwen2.5-VL**, so Chinese semantic alignment should be better than an English rendering —
   > this is a lever **never tried** in the first 21 rounds.

## Key conclusions

1. **A Chinese prompt has extremely strong controllability over "objects and scenes"**: the Boshan censer's incense, the screen's pictorial feel, the yingluo collar, gold-woven embroidery patterns
   and the jade ring waist ornament — all hit in one try, with noticeably higher precision than the English version (the English version often drops props).
   > **Chinese is suited to writing "what there is" (person, object, scene, state); English is suited to writing "what material" (glaze, translucency, microscopic texture).**
2. **De-figurinization and preserving the bone china texture are two goals that can be decoupled; this round wrongly tied them together**:
   after deleting the "doll / porcelain" vocabulary together, the model drew her as an ordinary real person, and the skin naturally became real-human skin.
   **The correct approach: the subject is a living person while the material is still bone china** — the two are not contradictory.
3. **Makeup needs a reverse constraint**: without writing "light makeup / bare makeup", the model defaults to heavy lipstick and modern eyebrow shapes, producing a "photo-studio feel".

## Changes for the next round (R23)

1. **Mixed Chinese-English writing** (the most important methodological correction this round):
   - **Chinese passage**: character / expression / jewellery / clothing / environment (R22 proved these controllable)
   - **English passage**: **material** — reuse the validated bone china glaze vocabulary from `BASE_Q10`, but with **the subject "her skin"**
     rather than "a doll": `fired glaze` / `delicate warm translucency at the ears, nose wings and
     fingertips` / `microscopic glaze texture with polishing marks` / `microscopic bubbles
     suspended inside the glaze` / `absolutely no CGI smoothness`
2. **Add reverse constraints for clothing/makeup**: `不施浓妆`, `唇上只点一点浅胭脂`, `眉如远山`, to suppress the photo-studio feel
3. **The final means for skin detail is still cropping**: crop a face close-up from the 3.43 MP base image and directly test whether "fineness" meets the bar
